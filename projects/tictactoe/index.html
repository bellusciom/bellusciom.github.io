Tic-Tac-Toe: $n \times n$ is Unwinnable

A notebook proof that generalized tic-tac-toe cannot be won against a competent opponent.

This argument concerns generalized tic-tac-toe: $n$ in a row on an $n \times n$ board, with X moving first. Two counting lemmas handle the classical $3 \times 3$ game, and an induction step extends the result to every larger board: the opposing player can always deny a win, so the game is decidably unwinnable. Throughout, "turn $n$" counts X's own moves, and win conditions are counted from X's perspective.

Work in progress. This proof is currently unfinished: the lemmas below cover turns 1, 2, and 5, but the cases following turns 3 and 4 are still missing.
Lemma 1. You need two or more simultaneous win conditions to win on your next turn.
Proof. Suppose after turn $n$ you have one or zero win conditions. Then O will either block the one win condition you have, or will go anywhere — so you cannot win on turn $n+1$. $\blacksquare$
Lemma 2. You cannot have more than two win conditions after turns 1, 2, or 5.
Proof.
  1. Turn 1: after turn 1 you will have placed only one X, and cannot have any win conditions.
  2. Turn 2: after turn 2 you will have placed two X's. However, no win conditions overlap in two places, so you can have at most one win condition.
  3. Turn 5: after turn 5 the entire board will be filled, so you cannot have any win conditions. $\blacksquare$
Note. A win condition is defined by having two members in a row, column, or diagonal, and the last spot open.

Induction step

Proof. Suppose $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ tic-tac-toe is decidably unwinnable. Then for an $n \times n$ grid, take some $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ subgrid.

If X plays within that subgrid, respond as if playing $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ tic-tac-toe.

If X plays outside the subgrid, play an O to block one of the two win conditions independent of the subgrid, in alternating fashion.

Then, because $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ tic-tac-toe is decidably unwinnable, there will not be any $n-1$ consecutive X's in the subgrid.

But with every move outside of the subgrid, one of the remaining two win conditions was blocked. So X cannot win outside the subgrid.

Thus, $n \times n$ tic-tac-toe is decidably unwinnable. $\blacksquare$
