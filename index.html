Michael Belluscio

Mathematician · National Institutes of Health · Section on Critical Brain Dynamics

I am a mathematician at the National Institutes of Health working in the Section on Critical Brain Dynamics in Dr. Dietmar Plenz's lab. My work in Dr. Plenz's lab centers on dynamical systems, cellular automata, and computational models of complex systems — specifically in the creation and analysis of neural networks aimed at producing glider-like collective behavior similar to that observed in cellular automata, with preprint currently available on bioRxiv. My interests are broadly fractals, chaos, and complex behaviors in dynamical systems, but in general I am curious about how intricate patterns form and what commonalities exist across cases. This page collects a selection of my computational and mathematical projects outside the scope of my work at the NIH with source code included where applicable.

Projects

Mandelbrot Escape Potential

The Douady-Hubbard potential of the Mandelbrot set, plus a map of Julia-set Hausdorff dimension across the parameter plane.

Driven Duffing Oscillator

Phase diagram of a forced double-well oscillator, with a live explorer that evolves the initial-condition space for any forcing and damping.

Hierarchical Sandpile Model

A multiscale sandpile automaton with stochastic cascades across nested lattice layers.

Generative Trees

Ideal tree outlines built from golden-angle rotations, continuous growth along outline with noise.

Automated Minesweeper Solver

Minesweeper generator and logical deduction-based solving algorithm.

Hybrid Lattice Network

An excitatory/inhibitory lattice with a fractal hierarchy of long-range links.

Tic-Tac-Toe Is Unwinnable

A proof by induction that n-in-a-row on an n×n board can always be denied by the opposing player.

An Analysis of Chopsticks

A complete analysis of the hand game Chopsticks.

Bubblegum Elimination

A proof that the chooser in the circle-elimination game can always direct the next choice back to themselves.
